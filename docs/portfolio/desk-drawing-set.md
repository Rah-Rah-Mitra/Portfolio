# Desk drawing set

The desktop workbench's desk shows Sample Town N5's engineering drawings — the
estate's floor plans, drawn sheet by sheet, raised into an axonometric, stacked
into the block and set into the aerial view through the poster's own camera. This
page is the drawing set's contract: where its line work comes from, what it may
and may not claim, how it is generated and gated.

Sample Town N5 is a generated sample estate, not a real town and not HDB's own
plans. Every sheet carries the credit "Sample Town N5 © Rahul Mitra, CC BY 4.0,
generated by Bonsai-Estate" and says what it infers.

## 1. Data

### Where it comes from

`scripts/drawings/build.ts` reads the committed estate pack and nothing else:

- `public/estate/v1.2/pack.<h8>.json` (found through `ESTATE_CATALOGUE.packUrl`):
  each building's footprint, its position `at`, its storeys' FFLs, `roofTop`,
  the licence and the aerial poster's camera (`views.aerialNE`);
- each building's `nav/<ID>.<h8>.json.gz`: rooms (outline, label, flat, clear
  height, open-air), passable doors, stair flights and lift landings;
- `site/ground.<h8>.bin.gz` (SN5G): the ground raster the kerbs are traced from.

Every stored file is checked against both of its hashes and its name before it
is read. Nothing is fetched at runtime: the desk never requests anything under
`/estate/`, so the Estate window's "a visit that never brings it forward
downloads only its poster" still holds (tests/e2e/estate.spec.ts case 1).

`npm run drawings` (`scripts/drawings/export.mjs`, Vite SSR, as `npm run
snapshot` runs lib/portfolioSnapshot.ts) writes:

| File | What | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| `lib/drawings/site.generated.ts` | the source header, credit, notes, the poster camera, the estate extent, each building's footprint, FFLs, sheets and roof caps, the kerbs | 12,808 B / 3,298 B gzip |
| `lib/drawings/sheets/<ID>.generated.ts` ×14 | each building's plan sheets (L1, typical, roof; the hawker centre's L1 and L2) | 7.2–20.1 KB / 2.5–4.8 KB gzip |
| `lib/drawings/sheetLoaders.generated.ts` | one lazy `import()` per building | 1,290 B |

48.6 KB gzip in all, of which a visitor loads the site module and one building
at a time. The types are `lib/drawings/types.ts`. lib/estate is used by the
generator only: the runtime drawing code imports its types and nothing else, or
Rollup would put an Estate module in a shared chunk (`vite.config.ts` names that
chunk `estate-shared-*`, which the Estate e2e forbids at boot).

### Format

Integer centimetres (stair flights in millimetres), x east, y north. Plans are
block-local; the site plan is the estate frame (block-local + `at`; the pack has
no rotated sites). A ring is `n, f, dx0, dy0, d1 … d(n−2)`: every edge of every
room and footprint is axis-aligned, so a ring is its first vertex (relative to
the previous ring's) and the lengths of its first n−2 edges, alternating axis;
the last two close it. Rings are counter-clockwise and stored in **pen order**,
the order the draw-in reveals them: the rooms outside any flat by distance from
the lift landings outwards, then each flat (plan.ts `roomGroups` order), nearest
room next, each entered at the vertex nearest the previous pen-up.

A sheet carries its rooms (`rings`, `codes`, `labels`, label `anchors` for the
named rooms of at least 9 m², `groups`/`units` for the flats), the open-air
rooms (`ext`), heights (`h` and `hx`), the space boundaries with no gap between
two rooms (`shared`), the doors (`doors`), the stair flights (`flights`), the
lift landings (`lifts`), the inferred lift cores (`cores`), the unroomed regions
too thick to be walls (`voids`), the open-air strips (`bands`) and the slab edges
beside them (`slabs`), the islands left out of the wall fill (`skip`), and the
car park's lot counts (`lots`).

### Derivations

- **Walls are not modelled.** They are the gaps between spaces inside the
  footprint, measured 0.10–0.27 m. The wall fill (poché) is one even-odd fill of
  the footprint, the rooms (less `skip`), the cores, the voids and the bands: what
  is left is exactly the thin unroomed space. The generator classifies the
  unroomed space on a 5 cm raster: a 0.35 m opening finds the thick regions; each
  must be a rectangle (area / box ≥ 0.98) whose sides meet room or footprint
  edges (snapped exactly), at least 1 m across. What remains must hold no 0.40 m
  square, or generation fails.
- **Lift cores (inferred).** A thick rectangle behind a bank of lift landings
  (the point 0.6 m behind a landing inside it), 1.5–3.5 m deep and 1.8–3.2 m per
  lift: blocks 501–506 and 512 have one, 507/508/510/511 two, 509 three, the car
  park and the hawker centre's L1 one. A roof with no landings keeps the core of
  the typical floor where it lines up ("aligned below"). Cores are left unfilled
  and labelled `LIFT CORE · n LIFTS (INFERRED)`.
- **Voids.** Any other thick unroomed rectangle: only the car park's decks have
  them (the 364.3 m² ramp box, x −13.2…13.2, y 36.2…50.0, and two of 9.3 and
  7.0 m²), unfilled, the large one labelled `NOT A ROOM IN THE DATA`.
- **Partial sheet.** Where thick unroomed space is over 25 % of the footprint,
  the sheet has no wall fill and draws the footprint dashed (`OUTLINE OF L1
  BELOW`): only the hawker centre's L2, which is its shop block alone.
- **Open-air strips and slab edges.** Between an open-air room (void deck, roof
  deck, balcony) and the footprint, within 0.30 m, there is no wall: the strip
  is unfilled and the footprint beside it is drawn as a slab edge. Strips from
  neighbouring edges can overlap at a stepped corner, so they are unioned into
  disjoint rectangles.
- **Shared boundaries.** Two rooms that meet with no gap share an edge: drawn
  once, dashed (a space boundary, no wall).
- **Doors are openings.** The data holds each passable door's centre on its wall
  line, its direction and its width — no swing, no hinge. Each is drawn as a gap
  between its two faces, the nearest parallel edges on either side within
  0.40 m, with a jamb at each end. Depths measured over all 854: 0.10 m × 455,
  0.20 × 323, 0.25 × 71, 0.26 × 3, 0.15 × 2 (the car park roof's stair doors,
  whose islands sit inside the garden's outline: the nearer edge is the face).
- **Stairs.** Each flight from its stored start, end, width, risers and riser:
  risers at k · going, stringers either side, a walking line, labelled `UP · 21 R
  × 171` (the sum over the stair's flights, the stored riser in mm; `DN` on a
  storey nothing climbs from). Every stair's flights rise exactly one storey
  (asserted to 1 mm).
- **Massing.** The footprint extruded to the roof level (the RF FFL), the storey
  rings at each FFL, and the roof's enclosed rooms (stair cores) by their stored
  clear height. Never higher: the pack's `roofTop` is the model's top
  (`engine.bounds_local`), shown as a `MODEL TOP` level tag only.
- **Kerbs.** The road edge from the ground raster: marching squares over the cell
  centres with "road" = at least 10 cm below the paving, skipping squares that
  touch a nodata cell (building aprons); 3,388 m.

### Never drawn

Door swings or hinges, glazing, furniture, columns or a structural grid, hatch
patterns, areas, façades, trees, parapets, massing above the roof other than
the roof's rooms, and a site boundary (the pack holds only the 400 × 400 m
extent). The car park's lot labels name their storey and are counted instead
(`126 CAR · 51 MOTORCYCLE LOTS PER DECK`). tests/drawing-set.test.ts pins the
sheet's key list and refuses any key named after a swing, hinge, glazing,
column or parapet.

### Gates

- `tests/drawing-set.test.ts` regenerates the set in memory and compares every
  file byte for byte with the committed one ("run `npm run drawings`"), checks the
  header against the catalogue, and pins the derivations above against the nav
  files (every room ring, 854 doors and their depths, the riser labels, the cores
  and voids, the slab edges, the massing caps, the budgets).
- `scripts/check-bundle.mjs` `checkDrawingSet` is the deploy twin (Vercel runs
  only `npm run build`): the set must name the catalogue's pack, edition and
  commit, carry the sha256 of the pack the build ships and the digest of the
  generator (`scripts/drawings/build.ts`, LF), and a dev set never builds on
  Vercel or CI.
- `.gitattributes`: `lib/drawings/**/*.generated.ts text eol=lf`.

**After a release re-pack** (docs/portfolio/estate-pack.md "Moving to a new
version"), run `npm run drawings` and commit `lib/drawings/**/*.generated.ts`
with the pack and the catalogue. A derivation the new data breaks (a thick
region that is not a rectangle, a stair that does not rise one storey) fails
generation rather than drawing wrongly.

### Budgets

Measured at generation, capped at about 10 % over (tests/drawing-set.test.ts):
the site module ≤ 14,500 B raw and 3,700 B gzip; each building's module
≤ 22,000 B and 5,400 B gzip; all ≤ 54,000 B gzip. (The plan's first estimate,
3,700 B per building, predated the slab edges, the room codes and the door
faces; the slab and L-block modules measure 4.3–4.8 KB.)

### Licence

The drawings are derived from the CC BY 4.0 estate data and ship in the site's
`/assets/*.js`. `public/estate/LICENSE.txt` ("Desk drawing set (derived)") and
THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md describe what they add and mark as inferred — the list
is `CHANGES` in `site.generated.ts`.
